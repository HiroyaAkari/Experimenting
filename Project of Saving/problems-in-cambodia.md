# Project of Saving - problems Cambodian people face

Source: Gemini, asked via the relay on 2026-11-15. Unverified, to be checked against real data.

## Money and debt
Cambodia has one of the highest microfinance debt burdens in the world. Many families take several loans to cover emergencies, get trapped in repayment cycles, and are forced into distress land sales. Low financial literacy and online scams also drain savings.
- Affected most: low-income rural borrowers, Indigenous communities, small-scale farmers.

## Jobs and wages
Garment and construction jobs are steady, but wages barely cover rising fuel, food and rent, so there is little room to save. Most people work informally without contracts or social security, and returning migrant workers face a tight job market.
- Affected most: female garment workers, informal street vendors, construction laborers, young school-leavers.

## Health
Public clinics often lack medicine and specialized care, so families pay high out-of-pocket fees at private clinics or travel abroad. One serious illness or road accident can wipe out savings and lead to predatory borrowing.
- Affected most: elderly citizens, uninsured informal workers, families without Health Equity Fund coverage.

## Education
Primary enrollment is high, but secondary dropout is steep because teenagers work to help repay family debt. Underfunded schools, informal teacher fees, and a mismatch between coursework and job skills limit upward mobility.
- Affected most: rural teenagers and children from indebted households.

## Rural life and climate shocks
Farming families rely on rain-fed agriculture, so harvests are exposed to droughts, floods, and rising fertilizer and fuel prices.
- Affected most: smallholder rice farmers and Tonle Sap fishing communities.

## Governance and land security
Weak legal protections leave citizens vulnerable to land disputes and predatory financial schemes.
- Affected most: rural landholders and urban poor communities facing eviction.
